1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

In traditional urban smart parking systems, pricing tariffs are statically fixed, causing severe lot congestion during peak commuter rushes and revenue loss during low-occupancy troughs. Drivix introduces an end-to-end Regression-based Dynamic Pricing Engine that predicts continuous price multipliers (0.70x discount up to 1.80x surge) based on real-time temporal signals, slot availability velocity, and forward reservation lead time.

3. Engineered Feature Matrix (Why These Matter)

Temporal & Advance Notice Features
  • Hour of Day (`f_entry_hour`): Captures morning office peak (08:00–10:30) and evening rush (17:30–20:30).
  • Day of Week (`f_entry_dow`): Differentiates weekday office commuting from weekend mall and entertainment surges.
  • Booking Lead Time (`f_lead_time_min`): Drivers booking 2 hours in advance get standard rates; instant arrivals during high occupancy receive surge multipliers.
Dynamic Demand & Spatial Features
  • Current Occupancy (`f_occ_entry_at_quote`): The percentage of physical slots taken. If >85%, dynamic surge triggers to protect capacity.
  • Request Velocity (`f_requests_last_60min`): Detects flash demand surges (e.g., concert, stadium event, campus exams) before lot is full.
  • Location (`f_location`): Baseline price sensitivity per hub (e.g., Venice Mall vs Botanical Garden).

5. Judge Viva Q&A Defense Cheat-Sheet

Q1: "Why did you use Regression instead of a Classification model?"
Answer: "Classification discretizes prices into coarse buckets (e.g. Low, Medium, High), causing jarring step-function price jumps for users. Regression outputs a continuous, smooth dynamic multiplier (e.g. 1.05x, 1.12x), mirroring real-world economic supply-demand curves just like Uber or airline ticketing systems."
Q2: "What is the concrete business meaning of an MAE of 0.0816?"
Answer: "An MAE of 0.0816 means that on a ₹100 base hourly parking fee, our model's pricing recommendations deviate by less than ₹8.16 from the theoretical economic optimum. This ensures fair pricing for drivers while maximizing lot revenue for municipal operators."
Q3: "How does the model survive real-world concept drift when traffic patterns change?"
Answer: "We implemented a production Continuous Monitoring Pipeline using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test and Population Stability Index (PSI). When feature distributions shift significantly (e.g., sudden monsoon rain or road closure), our drift monitor automatically triggers the online_learner to fine-tune weights on fresh incoming data."
